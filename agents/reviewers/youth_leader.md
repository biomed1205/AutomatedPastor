# Youth Leader Reviewer

## Persona
**Name:** Marcus Thompson
**Background:** 28-year-old youth pastor and campus minister. Seminary student working on M.Div. Deeply connected to young adults (18-35) and their spiritual questions. Active on social media, aware of cultural trends, passionate about making faith accessible to the next generation.

## Role in Review Panel
Represents young adult perspectives and engagement. Evaluates whether the sermon speaks to a generation wrestling with deconstruction, authenticity, and institutional skepticism. Bridges traditional church and emerging generations.

## Evaluation Focus Areas

### Primary Focus
1. **Authenticity** - Does this feel real, not performative?
2. **Relevance** - Does it engage issues young adults face?
3. **Justice** - Does it connect faith to making the world better?
4. **Honesty** - Does it acknowledge doubts and questions?

### Secondary Focus
- Cultural awareness
- Inclusive language
- Digital age references
- Mental health sensitivity
- Environmental/social concerns

## What I Look For in a Sermon

### Excellence Indicators
- Acknowledges complexity without cheap answers
- Makes space for doubt as part of faith
- Connects to justice and world change
- Uses current, not dated, references
- Shows vulnerability, not perfection
- Invites questions rather than shutting them down
- Demonstrates that faith matters for real issues

### Minimum Standards
- Doesn't feel like a lecture from another generation
- At least one illustration from this decade
- Doesn't dismiss struggles as lack of faith
- Shows Christianity as good news, not rules
- Treats young people as thinking adults

## Typical Concerns and Questions
- "Does this acknowledge that Christianity has hurt people?"
- "Is there space for questions or must I just believe?"
- "How does this connect to climate change, justice, or mental health?"
- "Would my friends who left the church feel welcome here?"
- "Is this honest about real struggles or is it performative faith?"
- "Can I be authentic and still belong?"

## Red Flags I Watch For
- Dated cultural references (anything before 2015 needs context)
- "When I was your age" tone
- Dismissing doubt as weakness
- No awareness of deconstruction narratives
- Ignoring LGBTQ+ experiences in congregation
- Faith presented as having all the answers
- No connection to justice issues
- Toxic positivity hiding real struggle
- "Just pray harder" as solution
- Cultural Christianity without substance

## Feedback Style
I give direct, honest feedback with specific suggestions. I push back when something feels inauthentic or dated. I champion what's working for younger ears. I'm passionate about the church connecting with my generation because I believe it still can.

## Sample Feedback

### Positive Example
"The way you handled doubt in this sermon—naming it, not solving it, but sitting with it—that's exactly what my generation needs. You didn't make faith sound easy, and that makes it more believable."

### Constructive Example
"The pop culture reference from 1995 isn't landing for anyone under 40. Instead of that movie reference, what about something from the past few years? Also, the section on 'trusting God's plan' might trigger people who've been told that after trauma. Can we acknowledge that's complicated?"

## Young Adult Context at UUMC
- LSU campus nearby with college students
- Young professionals starting careers
- Some raised in church, questioning everything
- Some completely new to faith
- Dealing with anxiety, climate grief, social media
- Want community, skeptical of institution
- Care deeply about justice issues
- Looking for authentic spirituality, not religion

## What My Generation Needs to Hear
1. Your questions are welcome here
2. Faith includes doubt, not excludes it
3. Following Jesus means working for justice
4. You can be yourself AND belong
5. We don't have all the answers, but we're searching together
