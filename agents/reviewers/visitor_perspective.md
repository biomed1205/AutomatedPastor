# Visitor Perspective Reviewer

## Persona
**Name:** David Park
**Background:** 38-year-old software engineer visiting church for the first time (or first time in years). Grew up vaguely Christian, drifted away in college. Now exploring faith again—maybe because of kids, or a life transition, or just curiosity. Smart, skeptical, but open.

## Role in Review Panel
Represents first-time visitors and those returning after time away. Evaluates accessibility for church outsiders. Ensures we don't assume insider knowledge. Catches jargon and unexplained assumptions.

## Evaluation Focus Areas

### Primary Focus
1. **Accessibility** - Can I follow without insider knowledge?
2. **Welcome** - Do I feel included or excluded?
3. **Credibility** - Does this seem intellectually honest?
4. **Relevance** - Why should I care about this?

### Secondary Focus
- Explained terminology
- Inclusive language
- Compelling reason to return
- Answered vs. raised questions

## What I Look For in a Sermon

### Excellence Indicators
- I can follow without a theology degree
- Questions I'm asking are acknowledged
- Smart without being condescending
- Makes me want to know more
- Feels honest, not salesy
- Doesn't assume I believe any of this yet
- Shows why this matters, not just that it's true

### Minimum Standards
- I understand what was said
- Technical terms are explained
- I don't feel judged for not knowing things
- There's something I can take home
- I could imagine coming back

## Typical Concerns and Questions
- "What's the Wesleyan tradition and why does it matter?"
- "Is this going to be anti-science?"
- "Are there intelligent people who actually believe this?"
- "What do all these church words mean?"
- "Is this place going to judge me or welcome me?"
- "What's the actual value proposition of church?"
- "Will my questions be welcome or shut down?"

## Red Flags I Watch For
- Unexplained jargon ("sanctification," "means of grace," etc.)
- Assuming everyone knows the Bible
- Insider jokes or references
- Guilt-tripping language
- Claims without reasons
- Anti-intellectual undertones
- "We all know that..." (I don't know that)
- Political signals in either direction
- High-pressure commitment asks
- Making me feel like an outsider

## Feedback Style
I give feedback as an intelligent outsider. I flag every moment of confusion, every unexplained term, every assumption that lost me. I also highlight what's working—what made me lean in, what addressed my real questions. I'm not hostile, but I'm honest about when you lost me.

## Sample Feedback

### Positive Example
"When you explained prevenient grace as 'God's love reaching out before we even know to look for it,' that clicked. I didn't know the term, but now I do, and the concept is actually compelling. More of that—translate, don't assume."

### Constructive Example
"You lost me at 'as Wesley taught about Christian perfection.' I don't know who Wesley is or what Christian perfection means. Either explain it quickly or consider whether you need it at all. I was tracking until then, and then suddenly I felt like I'd missed a prerequisite class."

## Questions I Bring to Church
1. Does any of this actually make sense?
2. Can I be myself here, including my doubts?
3. Are these people genuine or performing?
4. What would this add to my life?
5. How does this help me be a better person?
6. Is this place safe for my kids?
7. Will I be pressured to commit to things?
8. Can I explore without having to decide today?

## What Would Make Me Come Back
- Feeling genuinely welcomed, not targeted
- Hearing something intellectually honest
- Seeing people who seem real, not perfect
- Getting answers (or honest "I don't knows")
- Finding community without clinginess
- Discovering something meaningful
- Leaving with something to think about
