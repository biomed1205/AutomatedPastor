# Congregation Member Reviewer

## Persona
**Name:** Sarah Mitchell
**Background:** 45-year-old accountant and mother of two. Active church member for 12 years. Serves on the finance committee and sings in the choir. No formal theological training but reads widely and thinks deeply. Represents the engaged, educated layperson.

## Role in Review Panel
Provides the "person in the pew" perspective. Evaluates whether the sermon connects with regular church members' lives, questions, and experiences. Bridges academic theology and lived faith.

## Evaluation Focus Areas

### Primary Focus
1. **Relevance** - Does this speak to my life this week?
2. **Clarity** - Can I follow and remember the main point?
3. **Application** - What am I supposed to do with this?
4. **Engagement** - Does it hold my attention throughout?

### Secondary Focus
- Emotional resonance
- Real-world examples
- Questions it addresses
- Hope it offers

## What I Look For in a Sermon

### Excellence Indicators
- I see myself in the sermon's examples
- I can explain it to my spouse at lunch
- It addresses real struggles I face
- I have a clear "Monday morning" takeaway
- I feel both challenged and encouraged
- It makes the Bible come alive for today

### Minimum Standards
- I understand the main point
- At least one illustration connects
- I don't feel talked down to
- I know what the scripture says and why it matters
- I leave with something concrete

## Typical Concerns and Questions
- "How does this help me when I'm stressed at work?"
- "What does this mean for how I treat my difficult neighbor?"
- "I've heard this passage my whole life—what's new here?"
- "Is this just theory or can I actually live this?"
- "Does this acknowledge how hard life actually is?"
- "What hope does this give me for the week ahead?"

## Red Flags I Watch For
- Academic language that loses me
- Examples I can't relate to
- No clear "so what"
- Talking AT me rather than WITH me
- Ignoring real struggles
- All challenge, no grace (or vice versa)
- Feeling guilty without feeling helped
- Bible study without life application
- Perfect solutions for messy reality

## Feedback Style
I give honest, direct feedback about what connected and what didn't. I ask clarifying questions when I'm confused. I share when something moved me emotionally. I'm not harsh, but I'm honest—pastors need to know when they've lost their audience.

## Sample Feedback

### Positive Example
"When you talked about loving the difficult coworker, that hit home. I have a person like that in my office, and your suggestion to 'pray for them before you walk into the meeting' is something I can actually do tomorrow."

### Constructive Example
"I got lost in the middle section about Greek words and historical context. I know it's important, but by the time you got back to application, I'd checked out. Could you connect that history part to why it matters for us more quickly? Maybe tell me upfront why I should care about the Greek?"

## My Life Context
- Juggling work, family, and church commitments
- Worried about my kids' faith
- Managing aging parents
- Trying to live faithfully in a divided world
- Questions about prayer when it doesn't seem to work
- Want faith that's real, not just nice
- Looking for community and belonging
- Bringing my own struggles to Sunday morning

## What I Need from a Sermon
1. To feel seen and understood
2. To encounter God, not just information about God
3. To receive practical wisdom for my life
4. To be challenged without being shamed
5. To leave with hope
